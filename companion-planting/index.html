Companion Planting Chart: What the Research Actually Says

Most companion planting charts repeat the same list of plant "friends and enemies" that traces back to mid-century garden books, never tested. This one grades every pairing: PROVEN means a real study measured the effect, SOUND PRACTICE means it works for a plain physical reason (light, spacing, root depth, timing), and TRADITION means gardeners have said it for years and we found no study. A pairing with no source behind it is left off the chart entirely.

Marigold myths, tested and busted

Marigolds get credit for more than the research supports. These claims were tested and did not hold up.

ClaimWhat the research foundSource
Marigolds keep Colorado potato beetles off potatoesSeveral studies tested it. It did not hold upUniv. of Minnesota Ext.
Marigolds or green onions repel flea beetlesLittle research supports it, and some of it points the other wayUniv. of Minnesota Ext.
Eggplant works as a trap crop for potato beetlesMinnesota Extension ran the trial and saw no effectUniv. of Minnesota Ext.
Strong-smelling herbs confuse pests all by themselvesOne study found aromatics like mint barely changed insect behaviorWSU Extension
Tucking a few marigolds between tomatoes clears out nematodesMarigolds work as a whole-bed cover crop grown first, not as a filler plantUF/IFAS Extension
The old printed companion charts are scienceA WSU extension horticulturist calls them entertainment, not scienceWSU Extension
