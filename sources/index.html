Where these numbers come from

Every date and zone on this site traces to one of the four sources below. Nothing is estimated to fill a hole.

Sources

  • Growing zones: USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, 2023 edition from planthardiness.ars.usda.gov, read by ZIP code through phzmapi.org. Up to 40 ZIP codes per state, spaced evenly across the state's ZIP list, then counted.
  • ZIP code to state: the open us-state-county-zip dataset.
  • Frost dates and crop timing: the ForrestFinds zone planting calendar data set. These are zone averages, not weather-station records for your town.
  • Want the real number for your town? Use NOAA's US Climate Normals (they include freeze and frost dates) or ask your county Extension office.

How a planting date is worked out

  1. A ZIP code gives a USDA hardiness zone.
  2. The zone gives an average last spring frost and average first fall frost.
  3. Each crop carries one offset: how many weeks before or after last frost it goes outside, and how many weeks it spends indoors first.
  4. The indoor sowing date is worked out from those two numbers, never typed in separately, so the three dates in a row can never drift apart.

What this site will not do

  • Print a frost date for a zone we have no data for. Zones 1, 2 and 10 and warmer show "no data" instead.
  • Print one calendar for a state that spans too many zones. Those states get the zone table and nothing more.
  • Pass off a zone average as your yard's real frost date. It can differ by two weeks.
